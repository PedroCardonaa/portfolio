Íntegra, Explained: Why Puerto Rico's New ERP Is Hard and Why It Still Matters

In July 2026, Puerto Rico moved more than 60 government agencies onto Íntegra, a single cloud ERP. The first months brought payment delays, but the project still matters, and how fast the data and processes settle will decide whether it pays off.

By Pedro Cardona · November 17, 2026 · 4 min read

Disclosure: I work on the reporting side of a Puerto Rico government ERP project. Everything below comes from public reporting, and the views are my own.

What Íntegra replaces

For more than 30 years, Puerto Rico's central government kept its books on PRIFAS, plus a patchwork of agency systems. The Oversight Board has pushed for a single financial system so the government can produce reliable financial reports on time.

On July 7, 2026, Oracle Cloud became the official system for government finance and procurement. Officials launched it as Íntegra, a platform to centralize the finances of more than 60 agencies.

It isn't the first attempt. In 2024, the Comptroller found the Treasury Department had not gotten the expected benefits from about $32 million spent on an earlier system.

Why the first months have been rough

Go-live changed how every supplier gets paid. Invoices now have to reference a purchase order number created in Oracle Cloud, or they won't be processed, according to the government's transition notice (in Spanish).

Suppliers also had to validate their banking information in a new portal (in Spanish). Each step makes sense on its own. Together, they slowed payments while agencies and vendors learned the new system.

El Nuevo Día reported payment delays and risks to services after launch. The Oversight Board later granted a one-time extension covering $362.1 million in fiscal 2026 obligations, noting that not all of it was caused by the system.

My take: go-live is the starting line

Every big ERP launch has a rough stretch. From what I've seen on the reporting side, the pain usually comes from data and habits, not the software. Old records have to be cleaned up, and people have to learn to trust new screens.

So I'd judge Íntegra less by its first few months and more by three signals over the next year:

  • Clean supplier records: fewer payments held up by missing or duplicate vendor data.
  • A shrinking invoice backlog: suppliers getting paid on normal terms again.
  • Faster audited financial statements: the original goal, reports leaders and investors can rely on, on time.

If those move the right way, the rough start becomes a footnote. If they don't, the cause is most likely data and process, and both are fixable.

Why this matters beyond Puerto Rico

Governments and companies everywhere are leaving decades-old systems behind. Íntegra is a live case study in what that move really takes: data cleanup, training and patience. The payoff only comes after the hard part.
